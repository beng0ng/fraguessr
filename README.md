# Fraguessr

Interactive cobe globe of where 354 perfume ingredients from 116 countries come from, linked to 602 fragrances from 198 houses around the world.

The page opens on a chooser: **Explore** (the globe, ingredients and perfumes) or **Daily scent** (the Fraguessr game: today's famous fragrance, name its notes). An Explore / Play switch in the top bar flips between them at any time, and clicking the logo brings the chooser back. Deep links (`#i-…`, `#f-…`, `#play`) skip the chooser.

Look and motion: a warm parfumerie palette (ivory paper and espresso ink in light mode, a warm near-black in dark mode, a copper accent that turns champagne in the dark, and a quieter gold for details), with perfume and note names set in Instrument Serif. `motion.js` holds the shared motion: names that write themselves in letter by letter, staggered entrances, count-ups, and a fixed particle layer for sparks, rings, rising mist and a droplet that arcs between elements. In Explore, opening a note or perfume plays that entrance, its stats count up, the list cascades in on tab and sort changes, and the globe sends sonar rings out from the selected note (softer ones from each note of a selected perfume) with a shockwave right after the pick. Everything steps aside under reduced motion.

- `index.html` page, styles and import map (cobe 2.0.1, three.js 0.170.0 from jsDelivr)
- `app.js` globe, zoom/drag/pinch, labels, panel, search (Ctrl/Cmd+K), origin tour, deep links (`#i-bergamot`, `#f-chanel-no-5`)
- `data.js` ingredients (354; ingredients marked `accord: true` are notes perfumers rebuild from molecules, such as melon, truffle or civet, placed where the real material is famous) (origin, part, method, harvest, description) and fragrances (house, year, perfumer, note pyramid)
- `fx.js` the animated vignette for each ingredient
- `facts.js` five researched fun facts per ingredient, one per rarity (354 × 5 = 1,770), each with a title, an animation motif and a source URL; `LEGACY_TIER` records which tier each note's original single fact became
- `facts-fx.js` the fact cards: 46 canvas animation motifs, 10 counter gadgets, the scene engine (25 backdrops, 14 ways the ingredient moves, 15 particle types, camera moves, the rubber-stamp caption), the five rarity tiers with their entrances and auras, and `factCard()`
- `rarity.js` the five fact rarities and their labels and colours, split out so the page can count and label facts before the card code loads
- `sync.js` saves the collection to the player's claude.ai account (see Collection below)
- `scenes.js` the per-fact scene direction, by note then rarity: backdrop, how the ingredient moves, accent particles, camera, stamp caption, tint and counter style
- `models.js` three.js studio, lit like a product shoot: a procedural softbox environment for reflections, soft key-light shadows plus contact shadows where objects meet the surface, a honed-stone plinth, and micro-roughness and pore detail on organic materials. It builds a procedural raw-material model for every ingredient (spiral-petal roses, peonies and carnations, fruit, flowers, woods, resins, spices…), glass bottle per house with paper or printed labels, amber oil vial, plinth, studio sweep, and the lazy thumbnail renderer used by the list cards

- `world.js` cartographic layer over the globe: soft colored shading that shows where ingredients cluster (weighted by how many perfumes use each one), atmosphere rim, graticule and tropics, starfield in dark mode, ocean, continent and country names that appear as you zoom
- `game.js` Fraguessr. Play opens straight into the daily scent (below); the pin-dropping modes and multiplayer underneath are still in the file but no longer reachable from the page. The original game, drop a pin where a note grows, up to 5,000 points a round over 5 rounds. Modes: Notes, Perfumes (a perfume and one of its notes), Blind nose (no name). Scoring: a pin within 50 km earns the full 5,000, then points ease off with distance (about 3,100 at 1,000 km and 1,200 at 3,000 km). Each extra hint costs 15% of the distance points, and on the clock a fast guess worth at least 1,000 earns up to 500 more. Games can use an optional 60-second timer. Each round shows a breakdown card with a distance ruler, and results end with a copyable score. Rounds open with an intro card; your pin drops with a ripple, a glowing arc flies from your guess to the answer, then a burst, a floating score and confetti on near-perfect rounds. Results replay every arc. Motion is reduced when the system asks for it. Open it with the Play button or `#play`; `#play-friends` opens the game menu on Online room.
  - Daily scent (`#play`, the Play button and the welcome card): the opening deals the hidden notes out tier by tier while the name writes itself in and the family bar fills; a right note sends a droplet of its colour from the guess box into its slot, which flips over in a burst while a puff of the same colour rises off the bottle; a wrong one evaporates a drop, types out the new hint and makes the hinted slot glow; the end screen counts the score up and turns the missed notes over. one well-known perfume a day, the same for everyone (picked by UTC date from a shuffled list of 138 famous fragrances with 5 to 19 mapped notes, so none repeats until the list runs out). The page shows its name, house and year, a short description of how it smells written from the families of its top, heart and base notes without naming any, its categories (genre, leading families, masculine/feminine/unisex, decade) and a family balance bar. You type notes (suggestions come from every ingredient name and the aliases perfume lists use; a bare word like "rose" or "pepper" counts for "rose de mai" or "pink pepper"). A right note fills its slot in the top/heart/base pyramid, flies the globe to where it grows and deals one of its five fun facts, drawn by the same rarity odds as a blend (common 46, uncommon 27, rare 15, epic 8, legendary 4, over the facts not found yet), which joins your collection. A wrong one costs one of five chances and gives a hint about the most widely used note still hidden, building up on that note until it is found: its family and tier (the slot takes the family colour), the region it grows in (the globe turns there), the part used and method, then its letter pattern. Notes made from aroma molecules with no single origin (aldehydes, ambroxan...) are shown from the start. The game ends when every note is found, the chances run out or you give up; the end screen shows the score and a rank, every note's origin with arcs to the perfume house, the facts found, the full description, a copyable result and a countdown to the next scent. Progress is saved per day (`noteguessr:scent:YYYY-MM-DD`, with a `scent:streak`), so a reload resumes; "Try another perfume" plays a random practice scent that doesn't count, with a way back to today's. The close button returns to the atlas.
- Multiplayer, chosen under Players in the game menu:
  - Same device: 2 to 4 named players take turns on each round. A cover screen hides the last pin between turns, then every player's pin flies to the answer together and a board ranks the round. Ends on a podium with standings and a copyable result.
  - Online room: create a room for a four-letter code, or join one from the code box or the list of open rooms. Uses the artifact `room` capability and only presence (no events), so anyone who can open the page can play. Each player's presence carries name, colour and their pins (rounded to 0.01°, with hints used and seconds left); the host's also carries the game (id, seed, mode, round, phase). Every page rebuilds the rounds from the seed and scores everyone itself. Online games are always 60 s a guess; the host moves the room on, and a player still guessing when the host advances is locked in with whatever pin they had. Late joiners skip rounds already played. If the host leaves, the first remaining player (by peer label) takes over after a 3 s grace. Online rooms need the page opened on claude.ai while signed in; elsewhere the panel says so and same-device play still works.
- Testing multiplayer: `test-mock-room.js` (loaded before app.js on a test copy opened with `?mock`) is a BroadcastChannel stand-in for the room capability so two tabs in one browser can play each other.

The ingredient list is grouped by family into horizontally scrolling rails, with a sticky category bar that jumps between families and highlights the one in view. Fragrances can be grouped by dominant family, A–Z, year or house.

The land dots are finer than stock cobe: `app.js` loads the cobe source and patches the dot radius so dots stay the same size on screen at every zoom (32,000 samples, cobe's limit). If the patch can't load it falls back to the stock package.

Family spotlight: tapping a family chip under the globe lights up every dot of that family in a wave spreading out from its centre (the rest dim, its labels appear and its dots send out soft rings), turns the globe toward it and opens a card with the note count, how many blends use them and the top three, plus Isolate (hide every other family; it then reads Show all), Hide, List (jumps the ingredient list to that family) and a close button. Tapping the chip again clears it.

Welcome: the Explore card shows a small live globe of every ingredient rising over the card's edge, and the Daily scent card shows today's real bottle rendered in the 3D studio with today's name; the accent words of the title spread in like ink, and on first load the main globe grows in behind the veil.

Ingredient dots are not cobe markers (those snap to cobe's dot grid). They are drawn like glass beads: a soft contact shadow, a bright cap fading to the pure colour and a deeper rim, a crisp outline and a glint. `app.js` `drawDots()` paints them on the `#dots` canvas every frame at their exact projected coordinates: a soft coloured glow (additive in dark mode) under a lit, rimmed core, sized by how many fragrances use the note and faded toward the globe's edge. cobe markers are still used for game pins.

Loading: the welcome is shown by a small inline script before the app's code has arrived, and a card tapped that early is acted on once it has. The fact card renderer (`facts-fx.js`) and scene direction (`scenes.js`), about a third of the code, load after the first paint (`cardKitReady` in `app.js`); a card asked for before then fills in when they land. On a simulated slow phone connection (6 Mbps, 150 ms) the welcome now appears after about 1 s instead of 8 s. The globe stops redrawing while it is scrolled off screen, and in a game on a narrow screen it gives up height to the panel.

Accessibility: checked with axe-core on the welcome, atlas, a note page, the daily scent, and the collection in light and dark. The welcome is a real modal (the page behind it is inert and focus starts inside it), the search palette is inert while closed, the game puts focus in the guess box on keyboard devices, the brand is the page's h1, the layout has a main landmark, and muted text meets 4.5:1 contrast.

Run it with any static server from this folder, e.g. `python3 -m http.server`, then open http://localhost:8000.
`index.html` has no `<html>/<head>` wrapper because the artifact host adds one; browsers render it fine as is.

## Standalone website

`website/` (also zipped as `fraguessr-website.zip`) is the same app as a normal static site: a full HTML document with a favicon and description, plus the JS files and a `.nojekyll` marker for GitHub Pages. Upload the folder's contents to any static host (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages). Outside Claude the online multiplayer rooms are unavailable (they use the artifact `room` capability); the daily scent works everywhere.

Theme: the top bar has a Light / Dark / Auto switch, remembered in `localStorage` (`noteguessr:theme`).

Fragrance journey: "Collect the notes and blend it" on a fragrance flies to each note's origin in pyramid order, gathers a burst of petals, drops or chips into a droplet that lands in a tray of vials, then pours the vials into a flask at the house, swirls them into the fragrance's colour and reveals the bottle (`runTour` in `app.js`; particles drawn on the `#dots` canvas).

Fun facts: every ingredient has five researched facts, one for each rarity, 1,770 in all, each with a source link. Rarity rates how niche the fact is, not how rare the material is: common is the fact most curious people know, legendary the astonishing one you only find by digging. Each fact plays one of 46 animations drawn for it (a whale breaching for ambergris, a number counting up for yields, a moon rising for night blooms and so on), and a note's five facts always use five different animations. Each rarity has its own entrance: common slides in, uncommon pulses with sparkles, rare rises with orbiting lights, epic pops with a shake and a burst inside a shimmering border, and legendary flips over from a gold star card under a banner with rays and confetti. Facts are earned only in gameplay: naming a note correctly in the daily scent or a practice round deals one of its facts, drawn by rarity odds from the facts not found yet (weights common 46, uncommon 27, rare 15, epic 8, legendary 4, renormalised over what is left, so a note's last hidden fact always turns up next). Giving up earns nothing. The fragrance journey no longer awards facts; as each note is poured it replays one you already own, and its end screen says how many of the blend's notes are still to earn in Play. Discovered facts are stored in `localStorage` (`noteguessr:facts`) as `note:rarity` keys; bare note ids saved before there were five facts are read as that note's original tier. An ingredient page shows a row of five rarity chips (found ones solid, hidden ones dashed with a lock) and the count, for example 2/5 found · 37/1,260. Picking a found chip opens its card; picking a hidden one shows a locked card with the chance it turns up and a button to play the daily scent. Reduced motion shows the cards still.

Collection: the third list tab shows the player's own collection. Notes named in a game are stored in `noteguessr:notes` (`{id: times named}`; on first load it is backfilled from saved daily-scent logs and earned facts). The tab opens with two progress cards (notes found out of 318, facts earned out of 1,590) and a row of rarity counts, then a Notes / Facts switch. Notes is an album by family: found notes show their picture and five rarity dots, unfound ones a dashed ? tile with their country. Facts lists every earned fact rarest first; picking one opens its note on that card. The filter box searches the collection, and the game's end screen has a See your collection button. All of it lives in the player's own browser.

Milestones: 13 goals in the Collection tab (Notes / Facts / Milestones switch), each paying out one bonus fact at least as rare as shown: find 10, 25, 50, 100 and all notes; name every note of a scent; play the daily scent 3, 7 and 30 days in a row; find every note of one family; earn all five facts of one note; earn 100 facts; earn a legendary fact. The bonus is drawn by the usual rarity odds from the unearned facts of notes the player has already found (lowering the bar only when nothing that rare is left), never from a note still hidden in the scent being played, so every fact stays tied to something named in play. Milestones are checked after each correct note and at the end of a scent (`checkMilestones` in `app.js`); reaching one shows a toast in the corner with a See it button (it plays the card in the game's fact slot, or opens the note). The end of a scent shows the next milestone and its progress. State lives in `noteguessr:miles` (milestone id, date, the fact it paid) and `noteguessr:stats` (perfect scents, best streak). Facts earned since the Collection tab was last left are marked New there (`noteguessr:newfacts`), with a dot on the tab; a note with all five facts gets a gold border and star.

Account sync: on claude.ai the page declares the `db` and `user` capabilities, and `sync.js` mirrors the collection (facts, notes, milestones, stats, streak and the last 45 days of daily-scent logs) to one private document per person, `data/users/<id>/save`, which no one else can read. localStorage stays the working copy. On load and whenever the document changes it merges the two as a union (every fact, the higher count per note, the earlier record per milestone, the newer streak, the further-played game per day), writes the result back to both, and reloads what the page holds in memory, so progress from another device appears live. Local changes are written up 1.5 s after the last one, and right away when the tab is hidden. A viewer who cannot save (view-only access, or outside claude.ai, as on the static website) keeps playing on the browser's copy; the Collection header says which applies.

Fact scenes: every card plays its own short film, so no two notes animate alike. Behind the fact's motif sits a backdrop chosen for the story (night sky, bazaar, lab, palace, storm, aurora and 19 more); the ingredient's own 3D model then acts in the scene (dropping in and bouncing, sailing on the ship's deck, sitting on a balance pan, being crowned, walking the compass route, hiding in a magnifier...); accent particles and a camera move (push, pan, dutch tilt, sway) set the mood; and at the beat a rubber-stamp caption written for that fact slams down with a flash, a shockwave and a shake that grow with rarity (gold ink for legendary). Counting facts get a gadget that suits the unit: rolling odometer, balance scale, tear-off calendar, tape measure, thermometer, price tag, dial gauge, a "1 in N" dot grid, a magnifier for tiny amounts, or a growing pile. The direction for each fact is data in `scenes.js`; every (motif, backdrop, movement, particles, camera) combination is unique across all 1,590 cards, and so is every stamp caption. On an ingredient page the card has a Replay button.

Fact sources: in v27 every one of the 1,260 source links was opened and checked against its fact. None was broken; 31 facts the page did not fully support were rewritten or re-sourced and 7 more were reworded to match their source (the audit tables are in `research/fact-audit-v27.json`).

Notes added in v28 and v29: 66 more ingredients, each with five sourced facts and scene direction (fennel, shiso, palmarosa, citronella, litsea, tomato leaf, mignonette, cassie, camellia, zdravetz, pittosporum, khella, cucumber, nectarine, cranberry, tamarind, cactus, marshmallow, wheat, fenugreek, galangal, kānuka, spruce, laurel, camphor, cassia, driftwood, mitti attar, sea fennel, cut grass, ivy, gooseberry, cubeb, blue tansy, marjoram, hyssop, melissa, wintergreen, cabreúva, sweetgrass, lovage, black lime, cajeput, ravintsara, pandan, yarrow, plum blossom, then opoponax, copal, dragon's blood, redwood, deodar, asafoetida, wasabi, black cardamom, nigella, sunflower, cotton candy, daphne, passionflower, redcurrant, champagne, amaryllis, Rangoon creeper, sake and tobacco flower). Many link to fragrances that already listed them (fennel, cucumber, cactus, wheat, driftwood, cut grass, earthy notes and others), and Libya, Singapore, Afghanistan and Somalia join the map. `models.js` `eighthNotes()` builds their 3D specimens: close botanical cousins borrow an existing build (`LIKE`), and cucumber, cactus, marshmallow, tomato leaf, driftwood, mitti, gooseberry, black lime, redcurrant, champagne, sake, cotton candy and sunflower have their own. The research for each note (pin, aliases, sources) is in `research/new-notes-v28.json`.

Map and daily scent polish (v30): the explore globe is quieter. Where note beads would pile on top of each other (Europe at full view), the more used note keeps its bead and the others shrink to small specks that grow back as you zoom in (`DOTK` in `app.js`); glows are smaller and screened rather than added, the colour wash is fainter, the graticule is wider at full view, sea names and the tropics appear only once zoomed in, the how-to hint fades after the first touch, and the family legend fits one row. In the daily scent, the perfume house is pinned from the start and every note you find draws a glowing thread to it with a drop of the note's colour travelling along it (`scentPins` and `scentLinks` in `game.js`), so the scent's map builds up as you play; at the end the found notes thread home in turn and the missed ones appear as small specks. The welcome card's sample notes are never in today's scent, and the bottle fits its frame.

Rarity odds and the perfumer's lab (v31): each note named in play deals one of its five facts at fixed odds, common 60%, uncommon 22%, rare 10%, epic 6% and legendary 2% (`ODDS` in `app.js`), whether or not you already hold it, so each rarity turns up exactly as often as its name says; the collection's rarity row shows the odds. Every fact dealt, new or repeated, also pays drops of that note's essence: 2 for common, 3 uncommon, 5 rare, 8 epic, 12 legendary (`YIELD`). Drops are spent to blend any fragrance in the atlas: each listed note costs 1 drop in the top, 2 in the heart and 3 in the base (`NEED`, `recipe()`, `blend()`). The collection's Perfumes view shows your essence shelf, the fragrances ready to blend, the ones you have blended and the ones closest to ready; every fragrance page has a "Blend it yourself" card with the formula against your shelf. Blending spends the drops and plays the journey of the notes into the bottle. The lab is stored in `noteguessr:lab` as running totals of drops earned and spent per note and perfumes blended, so `sync.js` merges devices by keeping the larger of each; on first load, older saves are paid the drops for the facts they already hold.

**v32.** Globe labels now have a voice per kind of place (`labelStyle()` in `world.js`): oceans and seas in blue serif italics, continents in wide warm monospaced capitals, the largest countries in bold spaced capitals with a halo, medium countries in regular text and small ones lighter, and the collision boxes measure the text as drawn. Every fact card gets its own variant seeded from its id (`variant()` in `facts-fx.js`): a mirrored scene (never for scenes that show text), its own speed, a stronger tint toward the ingredient's colour, a hero size and position nudge and a different accent density; `scenes.js` lost its duplicate stamps and repeated scene combinations. The daily scent is played once a day: the end screen no longer offers a practice perfume and reopening Play shows the finished day. The collection's Perfumes view is grouped by fragrance family with a jump bar, a ready-to-blend summary at the top and, inside each family, ready first, then blended, then closest; the Notes view shows found/total per family in the jump bar and a progress bar under each family.

**v33: shared daily stats.** `stats.js` adds Krillion-style numbers to the daily scent's end screen once a Firebase project is plugged in: how many players finished today's scent, a bar chart of how many notes everyone found with your bar highlighted, the share of players you beat, your rarest find, the hardest note of the day, and a percentage on every note chip. Each finished game sends one multi-path update to a Firebase Realtime Database (`daily/<date>/n`, `daily/<date>/notes/<id>`, `daily/<date>/score/<found>`, all `+1`) together with `subs/<date>/<uid>` for the browser's anonymous Firebase user. `database.rules.json` only accepts the counters when that `subs` entry is new in the same write and each counter rises by exactly one, so a browser counts once a day and nobody can rewrite the totals. To switch it on: create a Firebase project, enable Anonymous sign-in, create a Realtime Database, paste `database.rules.json` into its Rules tab, register a web app and paste its config into `FIREBASE` at the top of `firebase.js` (it was in `stats.js` before v49). Without a config (and on claude.ai, where the Firebase SDK can't load) the panel simply doesn't appear. The SDK loads from gstatic.com at version 12.19.0.

**v34: speed.** The page no longer blurs what sits behind its floating labels, chips and panels (`backdrop-filter` was re-blurring the moving globe every frame); they use a near-opaque `--overlay-solid` fill instead. The globe, the map layer and the dot layer now render at an adaptive resolution: `watchFrames()` in `app.js` watches the median frame time and, when it stays above about 26 ms, steps the render scale down (1, 0.75, 0.55 of the device pixel ratio, never below 0.75 pixels per CSS pixel). On the standalone website the list thumbnails are pre-rendered WebP images in `thumbs/` (318 ingredients, 482 perfumes in light and dark), so lists no longer load three.js or compile shaders; the `<meta name="fraguessr-thumbs">` tag points at the folder and any missing image falls back to the live 3D render. `prerender.cjs` regenerates them through `?prerender=i` and `?prerender=f`, and `build_site.py` converts them to WebP.

**v35: smoother.** Glass beads and the colour haze under them are stamped from cached sprites instead of building hundreds of gradients every frame, and the haze is drawn at quarter resolution and scaled up. The atmosphere fills only its ring. The globe redraws every other frame while it only drifts on its own, and not at all behind the welcome screen. List placeholders breathe only while their thumbnail is loading. The 3D turntable in the detail panel turns for six seconds after a pick, then comes to rest and stops redrawing until it is dragged; its motion is time-based and its resolution is capped at 1.5x.

**v36: daily first.** The site opens straight on today's scent; the welcome chooser is gone, and links to `#atlas`, `#i-…` or `#f-…` still open the atlas (leaving the game now sets `#atlas`, so a reload stays there). The header reads Daily | Explore, with How to play and Your stats buttons; the theme switch is icon-only and search shrinks to an icon on narrow screens. First-time visitors get a short how-to-play sheet. Each daily has a number (`#1` is 20 Oct 2026). The stats sheet is built from the finished days kept in this browser (`noteguessr:scent:<date>`): games played, share of notes found, perfect days, current and best streak, a distribution of notes found per day with today highlighted, best and average score and a live countdown to the next scent. Notes now score by rarity, like a rare answer: a note listed by 60+ of the atlas's perfumes is a classic worth 100, 25+ common 150, 8+ uncommon 250, fewer rare 400, plus 50 per drop left on a perfect day. The share text carries the daily number, the score and a square per guess coloured by rarity (🟩 classic or common, 🟦 uncommon, 🟪 rare, 🟥 miss), and uses the system share sheet on phones. Sheets are `<dialog>` elements, so Escape and the backdrop close them and the page keys stay quiet while one is open.

**v37: daily bonus and a calmer panel.** A finished daily scent offers a GeoGuessr-style bonus: three of today's notes, seeded by date and spread across countries, to pin on the globe where they are grown, scored by distance and saved per round (`noteguessr:geo:<date>`), so a reload resumes it. Its total and squares join the share text as a 📍 line. The daily panel is simpler: title and one meta line (house, year, audience), a genre badge and three family badges, the profile prose under the hints, and no tags on the hero. On the standalone website the hero bottle is a pre-rendered WebP (`thumbs/h-<id>.webp`, made with `prerender.cjs h`), so opening the site no longer loads three.js; pressing the bottle swaps in the live 3D one, and a missing image falls back to it. The end card puts Share result and Stats right under the score; the plain-text share block only appears if copying fails. Explore the atlas, Your collection and Practice games sit quietly underneath.

**v38: bigger tabs, refined type.** The Daily and Explore tabs sit in the middle of the header, larger and on a raised card; Explore turns dark when chosen, and both stay readable when not. How to play and Stats carry text labels on wide screens. Names and headings use Cormorant Garamond and the interface uses Manrope (Geist stays only for the bottle labels drawn in 3D). The side panel is wider (480px, 448px under 1280px) with larger text, tabs, inputs and buttons. The globe's "drag to spin" hint hides below 1440px so it no longer runs into the region bar.

**v39: tabs on the left, Collection on the right.** Daily and Explore sit beside the logo at the top left. Collection left the side panel's tabs and became its own tab on the right of the header (`#tab-col`, with the note count and the new-fact dot); choosing it closes the game if one is open, lifts the Daily/Explore pill and shows the album in the panel, and Explore returns to the last list tab. **v40: share card and icons.** Links to fraguessr.com now unfurl with a 1200×630 preview (`og.jpg`: the wordmark, "Guess the notes in today's perfume, then find where they grow", three bottles and a "New perfume every day" badge), with matching Open Graph and Twitter card tags. **v41: country borders, smoother loading.** The globe draws the borders between countries (`borders.js`: Natural Earth 1:50m admin-0 land boundaries, public domain, via world-atlas, simplified to 0.04° and stored as base64 Int16 deltas, about 30 KB). Each point's unit vector is computed once and only rotated per frame, and the lines thicken a little as you zoom. Opening an ingredient or perfume on the public site shows its ready-made picture at once; the 3D model is built after the panel slides in, and its shaders compile in the background with `renderer.compileAsync`, so the model only appears once it can draw. The daily bottle keeps its picture until the live bottle is ready. The game's effects layer no longer clears a full-screen canvas every frame when nothing is animating. The site's `<head>` now preloads every module the daily page needs and the globe library, so they download in parallel.

**v42: clear continents, softer dots.** The globe now fills every landmass with a soft tint and outlines its coastline (Natural Earth 1:50m land, public domain, via world-atlas). `borders.js` carries the country borders and a coarse coastline (0.15°, landmasses at least 0.8° across); `coast-fine.js` holds a finer coastline (0.05°, down to 0.3°) and loads only once you zoom past 1.7×. Each line keeps a bounding cap so lines on the far side are skipped; points behind the globe are pinned to its rim so the fill stays closed, and the paths are built as `Path2D` and drawn in one stroke or fill each. Country borders are now lighter than the coastline. The land dots are smaller (cobe's dot radius patched to 5e-3) and lighter (map brightness 0.8 in light, 2 in dark). `make_borders.cjs` rebuilds both data files.

**v43: faster opening.** Nothing was removed; the same work now costs less. The 3D models' noise caches lattice values and whole cells, and canvas textures are filled a row at a time (`fbmImage`, splitting the hash's sine into per-row and per-column parts), so a model's textures build 2 to 8 times faster with the same look. `displace` welds vertices with a numeric hash table instead of string keys (same result as three's `mergeVertices`). Fitting the camera puts each sampled vertex into the camera's frame once and tests every trial distance with a few multiplies, giving the identical framing about 3.5 times faster. Together an ingredient's first open builds in about half the time (the slowest went from 300 ms to 100 ms). The 3D turntable stops drawing once you go back to the list. Panes only reset their scroll when they are actually scrolled, the category bar's scroll-spy and the list's keep-in-view step wait for the next frame instead of forcing layout mid-click, and the 482-row perfume list only lays out the rows on screen (`content-visibility`).

**v44: no stall or blurry stand-in when a model opens.** Glass and liquids are drawn through a transmission pass that redraws the opaque objects into a linear render target, and those shader variants used to compile on the model's first frame, freezing the page. The stage now starts compiling them alongside the normal ones (`compileAll`: the render-target and back-face variants, then it waits for every program to report ready), so only a tiny shadow shader is left for the first frame. On the public site the stand-in picture is now the model's own first frame at 864×600 (`thumbs/s-i-<id>-l|d.webp` and `s-f-<id>-l|d.webp`, rendered by `prestill.cjs` through `window.prerenderStills` under `?prerender`), sharp on any screen. The live model is built once the globe has finished flying, appears underneath the picture, holds that same pose for 0.7 s while the picture fades away, then starts turning, so the swap is invisible. The turntable also no longer jumps on its first frame.

**v45: an opening page.** A bare visit to the site now opens on a title page (`intro.js`, markup in `index.html` so it paints before any script runs): the bottle logo draws itself, the name rises letter by letter, soft motes in the ingredient family colours drift upward, and a slow reel of notes (picture, name and where it grows) slides across. Any link with a hash (`#atlas`, `#i-…`, `#play-daily`) skips the page, and reduced-motion settings get it without animation (the reel becomes a still row of six). The animation waits for the fonts (at most 0.9 s) so the name never changes typeface mid-rise.

**v48: things passing in the sky, and challenges.** Now and then something crosses the stage while you explore (`flyby.js`): a satellite, asteroid, comet, rocket, meteor or a rare golden comet at night; a paper plane, hot-air balloon, kite, butterfly, bumblebee or a rare golden butterfly by day. Each has a small chance at the end of a zoom (10%) or a spin (6%), and every 6 s while the globe is left alone for 9 s or more (14%); never more than one at a time, never in the daily game or with a sheet or the opening page up, and a 30 s lull after each (60 s after a catch). The first few carry a "Catch it" label. Catching one opens a challenge (`challenge.js`) whose difficulty follows the catch: easy (satellite, asteroid, plane, balloon) is one question with three choices; medium (comet, rocket, kite, butterfly) two questions with four; hard (meteor, bumblebee) three with a 20 s clock each; legendary (the golden ones) three with 15 s, built from rarer notes and closer wrong answers. Harder ones also cross faster. A challenge draws one of eight categories: where a note comes from, spot the note in a perfume, odd one out by family, name the house, which came first, top/heart/base (medium and up), whose fact is this (medium and up, with the note's name blanked out) and name the perfumer (hard and up). Win every question to earn a fact (easy: common or uncommon, medium: rare, hard: epic, legendary: legendary), preferring one you don't have yet about a note from the questions; it also pays essence for the lab. Catches and wins are counted in `noteguessr:sky`. Reduced motion: the thing fades in at one spot and waits instead of crossing. `?flyby=<id>` sends a given one by for checking.

**v48b: bolder flybys, rarer ones harder to catch, chances that grow with play.** Each flyby wears its difficulty's colour (easy green, medium blue, hard purple, legendary gold): a breathing glow on its head with a ring that pings outward, and a trail of sparks behind it; catching one throws a burst. Chances rise with engagement: every zoom, spin or tap in the last five minutes adds 1.5% to them (up to +35%), and past catches and wins add up to +25% more.

**v49: player accounts.** On the public site players can create an account (name, email and password, or Google), sign in, reset a forgotten password, rename themselves, sign out and delete the account (`account.js`, Firebase Authentication through the shared `firebase.js`). The header's person button opens the sheet; once signed in it shows the player's initial and name and opens their profile: the email and join date, whether the save is up to date, and their record (daily scents played, average share of notes found, best daily score, best daily streak, login streak and best, notes collected, facts found, goals reached, perfumes blended, essence drops earned, sky challenges won, perfect dailies). A signed-in player's collection is saved to `users/<uid>/save` as one JSON string and merged with the browser's copy by the same loop that syncs claude.ai accounts (`sync.js`, now `linkSync(backend)`, which also carries the sky-challenge tally), live across devices. Every visitor starts as an anonymous Firebase user (which is how the daily stats count them once); creating an account links that same user, so nothing played as a guest is lost, and signing in on a device adds what was played there. Signing out saves first, then removes the player's progress from the device. Deleting asks for the password again (or Google), removes the save, then the user. `database.rules.json` lets only that user read their save, and only a non-anonymous user write it.

To switch accounts on (this also switches on the daily stats): in the Firebase console create a project, then under Build > Authentication > Sign-in method enable Anonymous, Email/Password and Google; under Authentication > Settings > Authorized domains add `fraguessr.com`; under Build > Realtime Database create a database and paste `database.rules.json` into its Rules tab; under Project settings > Your apps add a web app and paste its config object into `FIREBASE` at the top of `firebase.js`. The config is public by design (the rules are what protect the data). Until then the person button stays hidden.
